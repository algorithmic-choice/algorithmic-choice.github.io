Abstract

What can an observed algorithmic choice establish? This second article distinguishes an observed selection, the relations mathematically determined by partial evidence, and the additional guarantees needed to justify and certify a strict preference.

For finite MIN/MAX evaluation expressions under independent-interval uncertainty (H1), including expressions with shared leaf variables, it characterizes when the available evidence guarantees that one alternative is strictly superior to another in every compatible completion. The necessary direction is established by constructing a single compatible completion through threshold clipping.

The study also distinguishes semantic determination from the soundness of an implemented verifier, illustrates the result with a twelve-leaf example, and discusses the epistemic and ethical limits of computational conclusions.

The central distinction: what an algorithm selects, what the evidence determines, and what a sound certification procedure can validly attest are different claims.

What is assumption H1?

H1 — Independence of the unknown values. Each unobserved leaf variable can take any real value within its own closed, bounded, nonempty interval. Once the evidence is fixed, every combination of admissible values for distinct unknown variables is allowed. Previously observed values remain fixed.

Shared variables are not independent copies. If the same variable appears more than once in a MIN/MAX evaluation graph—even in both alternatives—it receives the same value at every occurrence within a compatible completion. H1 concerns freedom between distinct unknown variables, not statistical independence.

This is the condition under which the paper proves the necessary direction of Theorem 1 using threshold clipping. The sufficient direction (separation of valid bounds) does not require H1. The theorem is not automatically extended to models imposing additional dependencies between variables.

In simple terms: unknown values can vary freely within their declared intervals, but a shared value must remain identical everywhere it appears. This is a mathematical modeling assumption, not a claim about how an algorithm searches.

Main scientific results

Theorem 1 — Strict dominance under partial evidence

Under H1, for finite MIN/MAX expressions, strict superiority of b over a in all completions compatible with evidence E holds if and only if the exact lower extremum of b exceeds the exact upper extremum of a.

[∀γ∈Compat(E):Vγ(b)>Vγ(a)]⇔LE(b)>UE(a)

Theorem 1 · Necessary and sufficient characterization under H1

The sufficiency of separating valid bounds is classical. The converse in the declared MIN/MAX model uses a common compatible completion and does not require disjoint subtrees. The paper does not claim historical priority over equivalent algebraic characterizations.

Other formal results

  • Selection is not demonstrated preference. An observed algorithmic choice does not by itself entail a demonstrated strict preference under a declared standard.
  • One valid counterexample refutes a universal claim. A strictly superior legal alternative disproves universal correctness relative to the specified configuration, domain, and objective.
  • Consistent evidence extension preserves determination. A relation already determined remains so when compatible completions are restricted consistently under unchanged semantics.
  • Certification requires additional guarantees. Mathematical determination alone does not establish that any concrete verifier is sound.

Research questions

These questions explain the mathematical result, the role of shared variables, and the difference between proof and certification.

01When can partial evidence prove strict superiority?

Under the declared finite MIN/MAX model and H1, exact lower and upper extrema provide a necessary and sufficient test for strict dominance in all compatible completions. The condition is semantic; its satisfaction does not automatically certify an implementation.

02Why do shared variables matter in the proof?

The lower extremum of one expression and the upper extremum of another may be attained in different assignments. Lemma 4 constructs one common compatible completion using threshold clipping, preserving the identity of shared variables and showing why non-separation defeats universal strict dominance.

03What is established by the twelve-leaf example?

Five observed leaves establish strict superiority in the specified tree. Removing any one observation permits both favorable and unfavorable compatible completions. This demonstrates inclusion-minimality of that evidence set, not global minimality across all possible certificates.

LE+(a5)=0.95>−0.30=UE+(a0)
04What separates mathematical proof from computational certification?

Theorem 1 characterizes a mathematical property. A software certificate requires separate evidence of soundness, provenance, admissibility, and correct correspondence with the declared comparison standard. The paper does not claim that any particular implementation has been formally verified.

Epistemic and ethical implications

What the computation proves — and what it does not

Correctness relative to an objective function is not, by itself, moral justification under an independent normative framework. This is a separate logical-philosophical distinction, not a consequence of the MIN/MAX theorem.

The research offers a basis for examining evidential claims in Responsible AI: distinguish observed outcomes, demonstrated comparisons, and certificates whose validity depends on additional guarantees.

Applications to formal verification, UCT/PUCT audits, and Responsible AI are discussed conditionally; the paper does not establish regulatory compliance or general algorithmic correctness.
