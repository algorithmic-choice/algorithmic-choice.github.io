What Does an Algorithmic Choice Demonstrate?

Computational Evidence, Verification and the Limits of Algorithmic Decisions

Nuno Valenzuela
Independent Research

Abstract

An algorithmic decision produces an observable outcome, but what does that outcome actually demonstrate? The distinction between selecting an alternative and establishing the validity or optimality of that selection is fundamental to the interpretation of computational results.

This research examines the evidentiary relationship between algorithmic choice, computational execution, search coverage and verification. It considers how the alternatives examined by a computational procedure constrain the conclusions that can legitimately be drawn from its output.

Particular attention is given to the distinction between an observed result, evidence supporting that result, and stronger claims concerning exhaustiveness or optimality. These distinctions provide a conceptual basis for evaluating the limits of algorithmic decision-making and the requirements for reproducible computational verification.

The central question is not merely which alternative an algorithm selects, but what the available evidence establishes about that selection.

Research Questions

What can be established from an observed algorithmic decision?

Under what conditions does computational evidence support claims of completeness or optimality?

How can verification distinguish a successful execution from a justified conclusion?
